Alert: When To Seek Immediate Attention

Certain Tinnitus Situations Require Prompt Medical Evaluation

If you experience any of the following, seek prompt medical attention or contact 911 for emergencies:

  • Pulsatile tinnitus (tinnitus that pulses with your heartbeat)
  • Sudden hearing loss or sudden onset of tinnitus in one ear
  • Neurologic symptoms such as dizziness, weakness, numbness, facial changes, or vision changes
  • Recent head trauma or acute noise exposure associated with new tinnitus
  • Severe emotional distress or thoughts of self harm

These situations should be evaluated urgently by an ENT physician, neurologist, primary care provider, or emergency medical service.

Understanding Tinnitus

Understanding Tinnitus

What Is Tinnitus?

Tinnitus is the perception of sound in the absence of a corresponding external source. It may be described as ringing, buzzing, hissing, humming, clicking, whistling, pulsing, or many other sounds. Tinnitus can be occasional or constant, mild or severe, and may be perceived in one ear, both ears, or centered in the head.

Tinnitus is a symptom rather than a disease. It can be associated with hearing loss, noise exposure, medical or neurological conditions, medications, head or neck injuries, and many other factors.

Why Does Tinnitus Occur?

Tinnitus is currently understood as the result of complex changes within the auditory system and central nervous system. It is not simply a sound produced by the ear. It is a sound produced by activity within the auditory pathway and processed by the brain.

Contributing factors may include hearing loss, cochlear injury or dysfunction, noise exposure, aging, medical conditions, medications, head trauma or blast injury, sound sensitivity conditions, stress and physiological arousal, and many others.

Is Tinnitus Permanent?

Tinnitus can vary widely from person to person. In some cases, tinnitus may improve or resolve. In other cases, tinnitus may persist over time. Even when tinnitus remains present, effective treatment can help reduce its intrusiveness and its impact on daily life.

Tinnitus does not always disappear. But its power over your life can change.

Can Tinnitus Be Prevented?

Not all tinnitus is preventable. However, protecting hearing from excessive noise exposure, managing cardiovascular and general health, addressing hearing loss appropriately, and avoiding known ototoxic exposures may reduce the risk of some tinnitus onset or worsening.

Is Tinnitus a Warning Sign?

In most cases, tinnitus is not a sign of a serious medical condition. However, certain tinnitus characteristics require evaluation to rule out underlying conditions.

See the alert section above for situations that require prompt medical attention.

Hearing and Sound Tolerance

Hearing and Sound Tolerance

Can I Have Tinnitus With Normal Hearing?

Yes. Tinnitus can occur even when hearing thresholds fall within normal limits on a conventional hearing test. In these situations, more detailed audiological or auditory testing may help identify underlying auditory changes not captured by a standard audiogram.

What Is Hyperacusis?

Hyperacusis is a form of decreased sound tolerance in which everyday sounds are perceived as uncomfortably loud, painful, or overwhelming. It can significantly interfere with daily activities and often coexists with tinnitus. Evaluation and treatment strategies exist for many patients with hyperacusis, and treatment must be individualized.

What About Misophonia?

Misophonia is a strong emotional or physiological reaction to specific sounds. It is a distinct condition from tinnitus and hyperacusis, though it may occasionally occur alongside them. Some misophonia patients benefit from audiological guidance, while others may require additional interdisciplinary evaluation.

Living With Tinnitus

Living With Tinnitus

Sleep and Tinnitus

Nighttime is often the most challenging time for tinnitus patients. Once the sound environment becomes quiet, tinnitus may seem more noticeable. Poor sleep can increase fatigue, stress, and tinnitus perception the following day. Treatment approaches may include environmental sound strategies, sleep hygiene recommendations, relaxation techniques, and referral to sleep or medical specialists when appropriate.

Stress, Anxiety, and Tinnitus

Stress and tinnitus can influence one another. Elevated stress and physiological arousal can amplify tinnitus awareness, while tinnitus distress can increase psychological and physiological stress. Recognizing this feedback loop is an important part of effective tinnitus management.

Concentration, Communication, and Tinnitus

Tinnitus can compete with attention, especially in quiet environments, during focused mental tasks, or in complex listening situations. Treatment strategies may include auditory rehabilitation, communication guidance, and behavioral techniques to reduce the amount of attention tinnitus consumes.

Emotional Impact of Tinnitus

Frustration, anxiety, sadness, fear, anger, and grief are common reactions to persistent tinnitus. Many patients feel isolated, misunderstood, or discouraged. These reactions are real and should be addressed. Emotional and behavioral care can be an important part of comprehensive tinnitus treatment.

Treatment Frequently Asked Questions

Treatment Frequently Asked Questions

Is There a Cure for Tinnitus?

There is not currently a single treatment that eliminates every form of tinnitus for every patient. However, that does not mean there is nothing that can be done. Effective, evidence based tinnitus care can significantly reduce tinnitus related distress and improve daily functioning for many patients.

What Should I Expect at My First Appointment?

Your comprehensive tinnitus evaluation will typically include a detailed history, appropriate audiological and tinnitus testing, discussion of your symptoms and their impact, and initial recommendations. In most cases, additional appointments are required to fully evaluate, plan, and implement your treatment strategy.

How Long Does Tinnitus Treatment Take?

Tinnitus treatment is a process, not a single appointment. Duration depends on the treatment strategy, the patient's individual response, and the goals of care. Progress is often measured across weeks and months, not minutes.

How Much Does Tinnitus Treatment Cost?

Cost depends on the treatment recommended, whether hearing or tinnitus technology is involved, insurance coverage, and other individualized factors. We are transparent with pricing and will review options with you before beginning any treatment plan.

Does Insurance Cover Tinnitus Treatment?

Insurance coverage for tinnitus evaluation and treatment varies significantly by carrier and plan. Some services may be covered while others may not. Our team will help you understand what to expect based on your specific coverage.

What If I Do Not Live in the Area?

Patients travel to our clinic from within and outside of the region for specialized tinnitus care. Please contact our office to discuss scheduling options if you are traveling.

When To Consider Evaluation

You May Benefit From a Comprehensive Tinnitus Evaluation If:

  • You have tinnitus that is bothering you, interfering with sleep, concentration, or communication, or affecting your quality of life.
  • Your tinnitus has changed, worsened, or been present for longer than expected.
  • You have hearing loss or sound sensitivity along with tinnitus.
  • You have been told nothing can be done and would like a more comprehensive assessment.
  • You want to understand your tinnitus and explore evidence based treatment options.
