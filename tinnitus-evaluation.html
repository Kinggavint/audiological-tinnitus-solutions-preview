Comprehensive Tinnitus Evaluation

Your Tinnitus Is Unique. Understanding It Should Be Comprehensive.

A conventional hearing test is an important starting point, but tinnitus often requires a broader view.

Our comprehensive tinnitus evaluation is designed to understand the sound you perceive, the health and function of your auditory system, and the impact tinnitus has on your daily life. The purpose of the evaluation is not simply to document tinnitus. It is to create the clinical foundation for a personalized treatment plan.

What We Evaluate

A Multidimensional Look at You and Your Tinnitus

  • Tinnitus HistoryOnset, characteristics, triggers, and changes over time.
  • Audiological FindingsConventional and, when indicated, expanded audiological findings.
  • Speech UnderstandingSpeech understanding and listening difficulties.
  • Sound ToleranceSound tolerance and sound sensitivity or hyperacusis when present.
  • Cochlear and Auditory FunctionCochlear and auditory function when clinically appropriate.
  • Auditory ProcessingAuditory processing concerns when indicated.
  • Tinnitus PsychoacousticsTinnitus pitch, loudness, and other psychoacoustic measures as appropriate.
  • Life ImpactSleep, concentration, communication, emotional impact, and quality of life.
  • Relevant HistoryMedical history, noise exposure, medications, lifestyle, trauma or TBI history, and previous tinnitus treatment.
From Evaluation To Profile

From Evaluation to Your Individual Tinnitus Profile

We bring your clinical findings, tinnitus characteristics, auditory results, symptoms, lifestyle factors, and quality of life impact together to create a multidimensional picture of your tinnitus.

We do not simply ask, How loud is your tinnitus? We ask: How is tinnitus affecting your life, and what can we do to change that?

Evaluation Guides Treatment

You Do Not Need to Arrive Knowing Which Treatment You Want.

You do not need to arrive knowing which device, sound therapy, counseling approach, or technology you want. That is our job.

We listen, evaluate, educate, and determine which treatment, or combination of treatments, makes clinical sense for you.

The evaluation guides the treatment. The treatment does not dictate the evaluation.
