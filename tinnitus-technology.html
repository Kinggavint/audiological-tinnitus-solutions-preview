Advanced Tinnitus Treatment Technology

More Options. More Personalization. One Goal: Life Beyond Tinnitus.

Tinnitus treatment has entered a new era.

Advances in neuroscience, audiology, sound therapy, and neuromodulation have created treatment options that were not available to tinnitus patients in the past. At the Tinnitus Specialty Clinic, we believe access to advanced technology is important, but choosing the right technology is even more important.

That is why we offer multiple tinnitus treatment systems rather than building our program around a single device. Your comprehensive tinnitus evaluation helps us determine which technology, or combination of treatment strategies, may be most appropriate for your tinnitus profile, hearing, symptoms, lifestyle, and treatment goals.

Diagnosis First

Technology Should Follow the Diagnosis, Not the Other Way Around.

It is easy to search online for a tinnitus device. It is much harder to know whether that device is appropriate for your tinnitus.

Different technologies use different therapeutic approaches. Some rely on personalized acoustic stimulation. Others use sound during sleep. Some combine sound with stimulation of another sensory pathway. New technologies continue to emerge.

We evaluate the patient first. Then we determine which treatment options make clinical sense.

We do not sell you a device and call it tinnitus treatment. We develop a tinnitus treatment plan and determine whether a device belongs in it.

Four Technologies We Work With

Four Advanced Tinnitus Technologies

Established Bimodal Neuromodulation

Lenire

Lenire is a noninvasive tinnitus treatment system that combines sound delivered through headphones with mild electrical stimulation delivered to the surface of the tongue. This simultaneous stimulation of two sensory pathways is known as bimodal neuromodulation.

The treatment is designed to influence the neural networks involved in tinnitus and reduce the impact tinnitus has on the patient's life. Lenire is used at home under the guidance of a qualified tinnitus healthcare professional following appropriate evaluation and fitting.

Why we may consider Lenire. Lenire may be considered for appropriately selected patients whose tinnitus characteristics and level of tinnitus disturbance meet treatment criteria. Our role is to determine candidacy, configure treatment, educate the patient, and monitor progress.

Established Personalized Nighttime Sound Therapy

Levo

Levo takes a distinctly different approach to tinnitus treatment. The system creates a personalized sound therapy based on the patient's individual tinnitus characteristics and delivers that therapeutic sound during sleep.

Because the treatment is designed for nighttime use, Levo may be particularly appealing to selected patients who want their tinnitus therapy integrated into the hours they are already sleeping.

Why we may consider Levo. Levo may be appropriate for selected patients whose tinnitus can be reliably characterized and who are good candidates for personalized nighttime sound therapy. Your tinnitus evaluation and tinnitus sound profile help determine whether this approach is appropriate.

Established Personalized Acoustic Therapy

Neuromonics

Neuromonics uses personalized acoustic stimulation as part of a structured tinnitus treatment program. The therapy incorporates customized sound based on the patient's hearing profile and tinnitus characteristics, together with professional guidance and tinnitus management strategies.

The objective is not simply to cover or mask tinnitus. The treatment is designed to reduce the brain's attention and reaction to the tinnitus signal over time, helping tinnitus become less intrusive in everyday life.

Why we may consider Neuromonics. Neuromonics may be appropriate for patients who can benefit from a structured, personalized acoustic therapy program and who are prepared to participate consistently in the treatment process.

Emerging Technology Availability Dependent

Tinniless

Tinniless represents another developing direction in tinnitus technology. Because regulatory status, availability, and clinical evidence for emerging technologies continue to evolve, we carefully review the scientific and regulatory information before incorporating new therapies into an individual patient's treatment plan.

Why we are following Tinniless. Innovation matters, but responsible innovation matters more. We are interested in new technologies when they offer a scientifically credible approach that may expand the options available to appropriately selected tinnitus patients.
