Tinnitus Treatment Options

Your Tinnitus Is Unique. Your Treatment Should Be Too.

There is no single tinnitus treatment that is right for everyone.

At the Tinnitus Specialty Clinic, we do not begin with a product, device, or predetermined treatment program. We begin by understanding you, your tinnitus, your hearing and auditory function, your symptoms, your lifestyle, and the impact tinnitus has on your daily life.

Your comprehensive tinnitus evaluation helps us determine which treatment options, or combination of treatments, may be most appropriate for your individual needs.

We do not fit you into a treatment. We build the treatment around you.

A Comprehensive Approach

A Comprehensive Approach to Tinnitus Care

Tinnitus is complex and can involve much more than the perception of sound. For some people, tinnitus primarily affects sleep. For others, it interferes with concentration, communication, emotional well being, sound tolerance, or the ability to enjoy quiet environments.

That is why our treatment approach is multidimensional. Rather than relying on one therapy, we maintain a broad range of evidence based treatment strategies and advanced technologies that can be selected and combined based on each patient's clinical findings and treatment goals.

Ten Areas of Care

Ten Areas Your Personalized Plan May Include

01
Education and Counseling

Understanding Tinnitus Changes the Way You Experience It.

Education is one of the foundations of effective tinnitus care. Understanding what tinnitus is, why the brain may continue to perceive it, and which factors can influence its intensity or intrusiveness can reduce fear and help patients regain a sense of control. Individualized counseling may include tinnitus education, management of triggers, attention strategies, realistic goal setting, lifestyle considerations, and practical techniques for reducing the impact tinnitus has on everyday life.

02
Sound Based Therapy

Changing the Auditory Environment

Sound can be an important therapeutic tool in tinnitus management. Depending on the patient's needs, carefully selected sound may be used to reduce the contrast between tinnitus and the surrounding environment, decrease tinnitus awareness, support habituation, provide relief during difficult periods, or create a more comfortable environment for sleep and relaxation.

03
Retraining and Habituation

Helping Tinnitus Become Less Important to the Brain

For some patients, treatment may incorporate principles of Tinnitus Retraining Therapy or other habituation oriented approaches. These strategies combine education and counseling with appropriate sound enrichment to help reduce the attention and emotional significance associated with tinnitus. The objective is not simply to cover the tinnitus sound. The objective is to help tinnitus become less intrusive and less important in everyday life.

04
Advanced Technology

Expanding What Is Possible in Tinnitus Care

Advances in neuroscience and medical technology have introduced new approaches to tinnitus treatment. For appropriately selected patients, treatment may include advanced tinnitus technologies or neuromodulation based therapies. These technologies are not appropriate for every patient. Technology should follow evaluation, not replace it.

05
Hearing Technology

Treating the Hearing System Can Influence Tinnitus

Tinnitus frequently occurs alongside hearing loss. When clinically appropriate, professionally fitted hearing technology can improve access to environmental sounds, enhance communication, reduce listening effort, and decrease the contrast between tinnitus and the surrounding acoustic environment.

06
Sound Sensitivity and Hyperacusis

When Everyday Sounds Become Uncomfortable

When sound sensitivity is present, treatment may incorporate counseling, controlled sound exposure, sound enrichment, hearing protection guidance, and other individualized strategies designed to gradually improve sound tolerance.

07
Sleep Support

Because Tinnitus Does Not Stop at Bedtime.

For many people, tinnitus becomes most noticeable when the world becomes quiet. When sleep is affected, the treatment plan may include individualized nighttime sound strategies, sleep environment modifications, education, behavioral recommendations, and referral to an appropriate medical or sleep professional when indicated.

08
Auditory and Communication Rehabilitation

Hearing Is More Than Detecting Sound.

Some patients with tinnitus also experience difficulty understanding speech, following conversations in noise, concentrating while listening, or processing complex auditory information. When indicated, treatment may include auditory rehabilitation, communication strategies, hearing technology, listening exercises, or additional auditory processing assessment.

09
Behavioral and Emotional Support

Addressing the Distress Associated With Tinnitus

Evidence based behavioral approaches can help patients change their response to tinnitus, reduce distress, and develop healthier coping strategies. When appropriate, we may recommend collaboration with psychologists, physicians, sleep specialists, or other healthcare professionals.

10
Medical and Interdisciplinary Referral

Knowing When Tinnitus Requires Another Specialist

Not every tinnitus condition should be managed through audiology alone. Certain tinnitus characteristics, medical histories, or examination findings may require further evaluation by an otologist, neurotologist, ENT physician, neurologist, primary care physician, psychologist, sleep specialist, physical therapist, dentist, or another healthcare professional.

Clinical Decision Making

The Difference Is Not Having More Treatments. It Is Knowing Which Treatment to Use.

The tinnitus marketplace is filled with devices, apps, supplements, sound programs, technologies, and promises.

Having more choices does not automatically create better care.

Clinical decision making does.

We consider your tinnitus, your hearing, your auditory system, your symptoms, your lifestyle, your goals, and your quality of life. Then we determine which treatment, or combination of treatments, makes clinical sense for you.

The evaluation guides the treatment. The treatment does not dictate the evaluation.

One Personalized Plan

One Patient. Multiple Possibilities. One Personalized Plan.

Your tinnitus treatment plan may involve one strategy or several working together. As treatment progresses, we measure meaningful changes in tinnitus related distress, sleep, concentration, communication, sound tolerance, daily functioning, and overall quality of life.

Evaluate. Treat. Measure. Refine.
